Background: 
Most academic programs focus on startups  OR corporates in isolation, leaving a critical gap in 
training students and professionals to visualize these two different kinds of important organizations 
working together for creating more impact . Bigger corporates interacting with startups might be 
seen as a paradox. However, the trend is reversing now and the more of these interactions are not 
for the sake of checklist completion. Corporate innovation literature finds many ways through 
which bigger established corporates can innovate and remain ahead of the curve. Co -creating the 
solutions with the startups is one of them. On the other hands startups feel that their size is not 
relevant for the corporates, and they fear the failure of getting overshadowed by the big corporates. 
In this paradox lies various opportunities which can help the startups and the corporates to come 
together. There have been examples coming from Indian ecosystem where startups from their 
incubation stages have been able to forge partnership with big corporates. Examples such as 
BioNTech providing its technical knowledge on mRNA molecules while Pfizer bringing years of 
experience in manufacturing it on a large scale and distributing it to the world during Covid19. As 
the students graduate from their programs major natural outlets for them are ; working with the 
startups, starting their own ventures, or working with the bigger corporates. In all these scenarios 
it is very critical that they understand the relationship, opportunities, and various frameworks under 
which an effective startup-corporate collaboration can work. Moreover, the existing courses being 
offered across BMU doesn’t offer it as a comprehensive package. The increasing importance and 
instances of more number of corporates working with the startups at the global level as well as in 
India makes it a right moment for us to introduce our students with these frameworks and examples 
from the ecosystem. 
Course Description: 
This course at the basic level will help the students to understand the need of the partnership 
between startups and the corporates. Why this is important beyond the current merger and 
acquisitions. Building on that understanding it will empower students to understand various 
models for these partnerships and defining features of these models. These models would be a 
basket of options available to both the parties right from providing access to facilities to having 
majority equity stakes in those startups. Through a blend of Indian and global case studies  across 
sectors, hands-on projects, and interactive workshops, students will learn how to identify strategic 
value spaces, design win -win engagement models, and navigate cultural and operational 
challenges unique to such partnerships. The curriculum will cover the full collaboration lifecycle—

from opportunity mapping and venture vehicle selection to execution, risk management, and

The course is designed with the objective of introducing the students with key concepts and 
frameworks for corporate-startup collaboration. Why these collaborations are important. What are 
the challenges and key success factors in corporate -startup partnerships. The course will g uide 
students in designing effective partnership strategies for innovation.

8 
 
Detailed guidelines for the evaluation components 
A. Industry Undercurrents: Where is the money? 
• Go to the GCV Powerlist 2025 website. (https://globalventuring.com/intelligence/2026-
gcv-powerlist) 
• From the list, choose 5 companies only. 
• From these 5 companies, pick 2 deals from 2025. 
• The 2 deals must be from 2 different companies. 
• No two groups should study the same deal. 
What to do for each deal 
• Read the deal announcement  and other details about it like news items, blog posts, or 
interviews about the same deal. 
• Study all the material carefully. 
• Try to understand why the deal was done. 
• Look at the business reason, the market reason, and the timing of the deal. 
• Compare the two deals and note: 
o What is similar? 
o What is different? 
o What kind of strategy does each company seem to be following? 
What the group should submit 
• A 2 page write-up, with a final comparison table of the two deals. It must answer  
o What was the deal and it happened between which companies? 
o What was the company trying to achieve? 
o Why did it choose that startup or partner? 
o What do the other articles say about the deal? 
o What is your final view on the real reason for the deal? 
B. Corporate Startup Tutor 
• Each group will create one Agent Tutor for the course (it must have access to all group 
members and the instructor) 
• The Agent Tutor will do two tasks only: 
o create a short class reflection after every session, 
o curate corporate-startup collaboration news once every fortnight. 
• For each class reflection, the Agent Tutor should help the group record: 
o key points from the class 
o One open question which they couldn’t get clarified in the class 
• For each fortnightly update, the Agent Tutor should help the group: 
o collect news items/ updated related to corporate startup collaboration. These can be 
social media posts (specifically linkdIn, announcement on the company websites, 
announcement by the startups, 
o summarize them, 
o and note why they matter for corporate-startup collaboration.  
9 
 
• The Agent Tutor must keep a visible prompt log. 
• The instructor should be able to see: 
o student prompts, 
o AI responses, 
o and student-added notes in the same shared space. 
C. For Project Report  
i. Identify one Indian startup or corporate that has worked with a corporate/startup partner. 
ii. The project must be based on a real field interaction, not only desk research. 
iii. The collaboration studied must be more than a client–vendor relationship. 
iv. Meet the organization, understand the partnership, and collect information through 
conversation and observation. 
Identify: 
a. who the partners are, 
b. what kind of collaboration it is, 
c. what each side gains, 
d. and what challenges they faced. 
v. The final project presentation will be evaluated based on groups presentation which must 
have evidence of interaction, such as notes, photos, or interview points. 
Use your own judgment to explain why this partnership matters.
